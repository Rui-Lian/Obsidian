## Examples of PAMPs

### **Bacterial PAMPs**
1. **Lipopolysaccharide (LPS)**: Found in the outer membrane of Gram-negative bacteria, LPS is a well-known PAMP that triggers strong immune responses.
2. **Peptidoglycan**: A major component of the cell wall in both Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria, peptidoglycan is recognized by pattern recognition receptors (PRRs).
3. **Flagellin**: The protein that makes up bacterial flagella, flagellin is recognized by TLR5 (Toll-like receptor 5).
4. **Lipoteichoic Acid (LTA)**: Present in the cell walls of Gram-positive bacteria, LTA is another significant PAMP.
5. **Mannose-rich Glycans**: These carbohydrate structures are common in microbial glycoproteins and glycolipids but rare in human cells.

### **Viral PAMPs**
1. **Double-stranded RNA (dsRNA)**: Often associated with viral infections, dsRNA is recognized by TLR3 and indicates the presence of a virus.
2. **Unmethylated CpG Motifs**: These DNA sequences are found in bacterial and viral genomes and are recognized by TLR9, which triggers immune responses.

### **Other Notable PAMPs**
- **Mycolic Acids**: Found in the cell walls of mycobacteria, these fatty acids serve as PAMPs.
- **Bacterial Nucleic Acids**: The presence of unmethylated cytosine-guanine dinucleotide (CpG) sequences in bacterial DNA is a key feature recognized by the immune system.

PAMPs play an essential role in activating the innate immune response by binding to PRRs on immune cells, leading to the recruitment of various defense mechanisms against pathogens[1][2][3].
