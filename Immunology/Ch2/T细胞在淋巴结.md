- **Activation**: Naive T cells are activated when their T cell receptor (TCR) binds to an antigen presented by an antigen-presenting cell (APC), such as a dendritic cell, in the lymph node.
    
- **Proliferation**: The activated T cell undergoes clonal expansion, producing many identical T cells specific to the antigen.
    
- **Differentiation**: The proliferating T cells differentiate into:
    
    - **Effector T cells**: These include cytotoxic T cells (which kill infected cells) and helper T cells (which assist in activating B cells and other immune cells). Effector T cells often leave the lymph node and travel to the site of infection to perform their immune functions.

    - **Memory T cells**: A subset of T cells differentiates into long-lived memory T cells that remain in lymphoid tissues or circulate in the blood, ready to respond more rapidly upon re-exposure to the same antigen.
