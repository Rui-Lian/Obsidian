- **Activation**: When a naive B cell binds to its specific antigen (often presented by a helper T cell), it becomes activated.
    
- **Proliferation**: The activated B cell undergoes **clonal expansion**, where it rapidly divides to produce many identical daughter cells, each specific to the same antigen.
    
- **Differentiation**: These B cells then differentiate into two main types:
    
    - **Plasma cells**: These cells leave the lymph node and migrate to the bone marrow or other tissues, where they produce large amounts of antibodies specific to the antigen.
    - **Memory B cells**: Some activated B cells remain in the lymphoid tissues and become long-lived memory cells. These memory cells can respond more quickly if the same antigen is encountered again in the future.
- **Affinity Maturation** (in germinal centers): In the lymph node's germinal centers, B cells undergo a process called affinity maturation, where the B cell receptors (BCRs) that bind to the antigen with higher affinity are selected for further survival and differentiation.

## Two step selection

### **1. Clonal Selection and Expansion in the Cortex:**

- **Location**: This occurs in the **cortex** of the lymph node, where naive B cells first encounter their antigen.
- **First Selection**: When a B cell's B-cell receptor (BCR) binds to its specific antigen, typically with help from helper T cells, the B cell becomes activated. This initial antigen recognition is the first "selection."
- **Clonal Expansion**: After activation, the B cell undergoes clonal expansion, producing a population of B cells that are all specific to that antigen. These cells are still **multiclonal** at this stage, meaning the expanded population of B cells has receptors with the same specificity, but the affinity for the antigen can vary.

### **2. Affinity Maturation and Selection in the Germinal Center:**

- **Location**: After clonal expansion, the B cells migrate to the **germinal center** of the lymph node, where they undergo further refinement.
- **Somatic Hypermutation**: In the germinal center, the B cells undergo somatic hypermutation (a process where their BCR genes mutate at a high rate), producing slight variations in the BCRs of the daughter cells.
- **Second Selection (Affinity Maturation)**: This is the second selection process, where B cells with **higher affinity BCRs** are selected for survival and differentiation. Follicular dendritic cells (FDCs) in the germinal center present antigens to these B cells, and those with the highest affinity for the antigen are selected for further differentiation into either long-lived **plasma cells** (antibody-producing) or **memory B cells**.
- **Elimination of Low-Affinity B Cells**: B cells that produce low-affinity or non-functional BCRs undergo apoptosis and are eliminated, leaving only those with higher affinity to persist.
